Every smart ring is a plain metal band promising the same sleep, readiness and heart-rate tracking, so nothing on the outside explains why one costs more than another. Four published specs account for the difference: battery life between charges, what the water-resistance rating covers, whether features sit behind a monthly subscription, and the size range the ring ships in.

## Battery life depends on what the ring is actually measuring

All three rings run a continuous PPG (photoplethysmography) sensor that samples heart rate, temperature and movement in the background, and battery life on a band this small comes down to how often that sensor wakes and how much cell fits inside a comfortable housing. Per-charge figures range from 4-6 days (Ultrahuman) to 10-12 days (RingConn), and two manufacturers also publish a charging-case figure — up to 150 days total for RingConn, roughly a month for Oura. The case number matters most for travel; the per-charge number matters most for whether the ring survives the nights you forget to take it off. Where it stops mattering: charge nightly out of habit and the published ceiling is theoretical.

## Water resistance ratings describe depth and duration, not just "waterproof"

All three brands publish a 100m (328 ft) figure, which reads like a dive-watch spec but is really a durability margin — none of these are marketed as dive instruments. The detail that differs is duration: RingConn and Oura publish a straight IP68 rating, implying continuous tolerance, while Ultrahuman caps its 100m figure at 12 hours of exposure. That changes what "shower with it on" or "swim laps in it" means for warranty purposes, even though the depth number looks identical on paper. Where it stops mattering: ordinary daily contact — showers, hand-washing, rain — sits well inside all three ratings.

## The subscription model decides what you get without paying every month

This is the least visible spec on a shelf and the one that changes total ownership the most. RingConn and Ultrahuman both publish a one-time-purchase structure: the hardware includes full access to whatever the app measures, no recurring fee. Oura's membership documentation states that without an active membership, the app is limited to three daily scores (Readiness, Activity, Sleep), battery level, and basic settings — deeper trend data sits behind the paid tier. These are feature-gating structures manufacturers publish directly, not prices. Where it stops mattering: if a daily readiness number is all you want, Oura's unlocked tier already covers that.

## Available sizing decides whether you can wear it at all

A smart ring is a single closed band, not an adjustable strap, so the published size range is a hard constraint rather than a preference. RingConn ships sizes 6-14, Ultrahuman starts a size smaller at 5 and also runs to 14, and Oura Ring 5 covers 6-13 — narrower at both ends. Weight differences across all three are marginal, roughly 2 to 3.6 grams depending on size, and unlikely to register on the finger; sizing accuracy matters more than the gram figure. Where it stops mattering: once your size falls inside the published range, the exact number has no further effect on daily wear.

## Matching the specs to your routine

- **You don't want a recurring app fee** — check the subscription-model column first; two of the three publish a one-time-purchase structure.
- **Your finger size runs small or falls between standard sizes** — sizing range is the binding constraint, and it varies by a full size at the low end.
- **You travel without reliable outlet access** — the battery-plus-case figure matters more than the per-charge number alone.
- **You shower or swim with the ring most days** — check whether the rating carries a stated duration cap or is an unqualified IP68 figure.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do I need a subscription for a smart ring to work at all?**

It depends on the brand, not the category. RingConn and Ultrahuman both publish a one-time-purchase model with no recurring fee for core tracking. Oura's own support documentation states that without an active membership, the app is limited to three daily scores, ring battery status, and basic settings — full trend data requires the paid tier. Check the manufacturer's membership terms before buying; this detail affects total ownership more than almost anything else on the spec sheet.

**Can I resize a smart ring after I buy it?**

No — a smart ring is a single closed band, not a linked strap, so there's no way to resize it at home the way a watch band adjusts. That's why every brand ships a sizing kit before you order the real ring, and it's worth wearing the sizer for a full day, since fingers swell later in the day and in warm weather — something the spec table can't capture.

**Does a 100m water-resistance rating mean I can wear it in the ocean or a hot tub?**

The rating and the recommended use aren't the same thing. Manufacturers publish the depth figure as a durability margin against splashes, showers and swimming, not a scuba certification, and none of the three brands market their ring as a dive tool. Ultrahuman caps its published rating at 12 hours of exposure — a duration limit a long ocean swim or hot tub soak can reach even though the depth figure looks identical to the others on paper.
