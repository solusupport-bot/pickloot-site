A weighted blanket is a simple idea — added weight that some people find calming for sleep — but the execution varies a lot between brands, mainly in how the weight is distributed and whether the fabric sleeps hot or cool. We compared three at different price points and with different fill approaches.

## How to pick a weight

The general sizing guideline is roughly 10% of your body weight: a 150 lb person typically does well with a 15 lb blanket, a 200 lb person with 20 lb. Going heavier isn't automatically better — too much weight can feel restrictive rather than calming, so it's worth sizing down if you're between two options.

## Beads vs. fabric-only weight

Most weighted blankets use glass microbeads sewn into small pockets to distribute weight evenly. The Bearaby Tree Napper takes a different approach — the weight comes from the knit fabric itself, which means no beads to feel lumpy over time and noticeably better breathability, at a higher price point.

## If you sleep hot

Bamboo and Tencel-based fabrics (the YnM and Bearaby picks here) are generally better choices than plain cotton if you tend to overheat at night. The tradeoff with bamboo specifically is that it can feel a little clammy in humid conditions, which the open-knit Tencel design avoids.

## Bottom line

Start with the YnM if you're not sure a weighted blanket is for you and want to test the concept without a big investment. Move up to the Baloo for a more premium, durable everyday blanket, and to the Bearaby specifically if you run hot at night and beads-in-fabric blankets have felt too warm for you in the past.
