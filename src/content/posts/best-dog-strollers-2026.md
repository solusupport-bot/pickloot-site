Dog strollers photographed side by side look like one product at three prices, and the copy leans on adjectives — sturdy, all-terrain, easy-fold — more than on numbers. Four published specs account for most of the real difference: the weight the frame is rated to carry, the compartment the dog actually sits in, what the empty stroller weighs, and how it collapses. Here is what each one changes.

## The weight rating is a frame limit, not a guideline

The spread across these three is wide. Pet Gear's instruction sheet for the Happy Trails Lite puts the maximum at 25 lb, Petmate lists the Gen7Pets Promenade at 50 lb, and WonderFold rates the Hudson at 66 lb in the medium size and 99 lb in the large. That figure describes what the frame, fabric floor and wheel bearings were built around — not a soft target.

Two things complicate it. Retailer listings do not always match the maker — Chewy shows 35 lb for the Happy Trails Lite against Pet Gear's own 25 lb, and the manufacturer's figure is the one the warranty is written against. And weigh your dog rather than estimating: a beagle at the top of its breed range and one at the bottom land on opposite sides of the 25 lb line.

## Interior dimensions decide whether your dog lies down

The weight rating tells you the stroller can hold your dog. The interior tells you whether your dog will settle in it. A dog that can only sit upright tends to shift and lean the whole way; one that can lie on its side usually stops moving, which is the difference between extending a walk and cutting it short.

Measure your dog from chest to base of tail lying down, then add a few inches. Pet Gear's manual gives 22 x 10 x 19 in, though its own product page lists 22 x 11 x 17 in for the same model — a reason to measure generously. Petmate publishes 25 x 13 x 21.5 in for the Promenade. WonderFold publishes Hudson cabin dimensions only as diagram images, not as text, so it cannot be compared on this axis before buying.

## Empty weight is the spec you feel on every trip

This is the figure buyers skip and then live with. The stroller has to come out of a car, up a kerb and sometimes up stairs, usually while you are holding a leash. The published empty weights here are 15 lb, 20 lb, and 22.5–27 lb.

That reads as a narrow spread until it meets a routine. Lifting 15 lb into a hatchback once a day is unremarkable; lifting 27 lb into an SUV twice a day, plus the dog, is not. Note the tension too: the lightest model here has the lowest weight rating, so the two specs pull against each other.

## How it folds matters more than how small it folds

None of the three publish folded dimensions. Worth saying plainly: if boot clearance is your binding constraint, you will have to measure a folded stroller yourself. What they do publish is the mechanism — Pet Gear an "Easy Fold", Petmate a one-button flat fold, WonderFold a FLATFOLD that stands on its own with the wheels folding in.

Wheels are published the same loose way. Pet Gear gives sizes, 6 in front and 7 in rear; Gen7Pets describes an all-terrain convertible front wheel with shock absorbers, and WonderFold a 360° swivel front pair with a rear brake, neither with a diameter. Larger wheels cross kerbs and grass with less effort; swivel wheels turn more easily indoors. With diameters unpublished on two of three, type is the only honest comparison.

## Matching the specs to your routine

- **Your dog is near a published limit** — the weight rating decides first; everything else is a preference you can trade.
- **The stroller lives in a car boot** — empty weight and the fold mechanism, and plan to measure the folded size yourself.
- **Long outings where the dog should sleep** — interior length, measured against your dog lying down rather than sitting.
- **Kerbs and grass rather than smooth floors** — wheel size and suspension, published as figures on only one of these three.
- **You push one-handed while holding a leash** — the canopy opening and the fold, since those are what you operate while occupied.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are dog strollers only for small dogs?**

No. Published capacities in this set run from 25 lb up to 99 lb for the large Hudson, so large-breed models exist. Capacity is the first spec to check, since exceeding it stresses the frame and wheels over time. Strollers rated for heavier dogs are correspondingly bigger and heavier empty.

**When does a dog need a stroller rather than a leash walk?**

Common reasons are recovery from surgery, arthritis or joint pain in a senior dog, short legs that cannot keep pace on a long walk, and pavement too hot for paws. For many owners it works as a mobility aid rather than a luxury.

**What is the difference between a no-zip and a zippered canopy?**

A no-zip design uses a lift-up panel instead of a zipper track, which is quicker for one-handed access and has one fewer part that can jam. Zippered canopies tend to seal better against wind and rain — a trade between access speed and weather sealing.
