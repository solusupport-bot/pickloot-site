Nintendo Switch 2 sales have kept climbing since launch, and a fresh console usually means a fresh shopping list: something to protect it, something more comfortable to hold than stock Joy-Cons, and something to get it looking sharp on a TV. Based on owner reviews and published specs, we compared three accessories that cover those three needs at three different price points.

## tomtoc Slim Carrying Case for Nintendo Switch 2

For anyone who just unboxed a Switch 2, a case is usually the first purchase, and this one is built specifically around the console's slightly larger dimensions rather than a generic pouch. It's a hard-shell case with a zip closure, cutouts for the console docked in its Joy-Cons, and slots for a handful of game cards, so it works for a commute or a short trip without adding much bulk. Reviewers have pointed to the slim profile as the main selling point over bulkier alternatives — it fits into a backpack pocket instead of needing its own bag. The tradeoff is that a slim case means limited padding: it's built to prevent scratches and light bumps in transit, not to survive being dropped from height, and there's no extra room to stash a charging dock or cables alongside the console.

## 8BitDo Ultimate 2 Bluetooth Wireless Controller

Stock Joy-Cons are compact by design, which makes longer sessions less comfortable for players with larger hands — that's the gap this controller is built to fill. It uses Hall-effect analog sticks, a magnetic-sensor design that's less prone to the drift that eventually affects traditional potentiometer sticks, along with remappable back buttons and swappable trigger modules for different game genres. Because it connects over Bluetooth, it also works as a general-purpose controller for a PC, which owner reviews call out as a nice bonus if you already game on both. It's noticeably heavier than Joy-Cons, which is a plus for grip comfort but a minus for portability, and being third-party hardware, it doesn't support every Switch-specific feature, including amiibo tap functionality.

## JSAUX 7-in-1 Docking Station for Nintendo Switch 2

For anyone setting the console up as a semi-permanent TV companion, this dock aims to do more than Nintendo's official one for less money. Specs list 4K output at up to 120Hz, plus extra USB-A and USB-C ports and a card reader built into the housing, so it can replace a small pile of separate adapters. Coverage of the dock has noted it undercuts the cost of assembling the same functionality from official Nintendo accessories, which is the main argument for it. The design packs a lot into one unit, though, and some reviews describe the port layout and styling as busier and less minimal than Nintendo's stock dock — worth knowing if a clean look next to the TV matters as much as the extra ports.

## Bottom line

The case is the easy first buy for anyone protecting a new console on the go, the controller is worth it once stock Joy-Cons start feeling cramped during longer sessions, and the dock makes the most sense for a dedicated TV setup where the extra ports replace other adapters. None of these are required to enjoy a Switch 2, but each solves a specific, common complaint owners run into after the first few weeks.

## Frequently asked questions

**Is a third-party case as protective as an official Nintendo one?**
Nintendo doesn't currently sell an official hard case for the Switch 2, so most owners are choosing between third-party options anyway. Look for a hard-shell design with dedicated slots for the console and game cards, since those hold up better in a bag than a simple sleeve.

**Will a third-party controller work with every Switch 2 game?**
Bluetooth controllers like the one above generally work across the Switch 2's library for standard button input, but console-specific features tied to Nintendo's own hardware, such as amiibo scanning or certain motion-control implementations, may not carry over. Check a specific game's control requirements if that matters to you.

**Do I need a dock if I mostly play in handheld mode?**
No — the Switch 2 is fully playable undocked, and a dock only matters if you want to output to a TV. If handheld is your main mode, the case and controller are the more useful upgrades, and a dock can wait until you actually want the console hooked up to a screen.
