A smart thermostat's product photo doesn't tell you whether it will install on your existing wiring, let alone what it can sense once it's up. Four published specs do that work: the wiring it requires, whether it has a display, which sensors are built in, and which voice assistants and networks it supports. This guide walks through what each spec changes, then lines up three thermostats so you can match the numbers to your own house.

## Wiring requirement decides whether this is a drop-in swap or a bigger job

Most older systems use a 24V low-voltage control wire set, and a "C-wire" (common wire) is the one that supplies continuous power to a smart thermostat's display and radio. Some homes have one sitting unused behind the old thermostat; many older setups don't. The Amazon Smart Thermostat requires a C-wire outright — no C-wire means buying a separate adapter kit first, and even then it's only rated for air-to-air or geothermal heat pumps, not gas or oil backup heat. The Nest is built to run without a C-wire at all, which is the main reason it's marketed as installing on systems the other two can't. The ecobee Premium also calls for a C-wire, but ships with a Power Extender Kit that draws power through the existing wiring when one isn't present — a published workaround, not a hard requirement.

## Display is a usability spec, not just a design choice

Whether a thermostat has a screen — and what's on it — changes how you interact with it day to day, not just how it looks on the wall. The Amazon Smart Thermostat publishes no display at all; every adjustment happens through the Alexa app or a paired Alexa device. The Nest carries a 2.4-inch QVGA IPS touch display at 240x320 resolution, enough to show temperature and a basic menu without opening an app. The ecobee Premium's 4-inch, 540x540 full-color touchscreen is the largest and highest-resolution of the three, and it's the surface used for its on-device menus and occupancy prompts.

## Built-in sensors change what the thermostat can act on, not just what it shows

A sensor only matters if the thermostat uses it to do something. The Nest's built-in motion, humidity, ambient light and touch sensors let it dim its display at night and adjust humidity-related comfort settings without add-on hardware. The ecobee Premium goes further on paper: temperature, humidity, proximity, occupancy, and air quality (VOC and eCO2) sensors are all built into the thermostat, and it ships with one additional SmartSensor meant for a different room, so the system can read temperature and occupancy somewhere other than the hallway the thermostat usually sits in. The Amazon Smart Thermostat publishes no built-in sensors beyond basic temperature control.

## Voice assistant and connectivity specs decide whether you need a separate smart speaker

"Works with Alexa" and "built-in Alexa" are different published claims. The Amazon Smart Thermostat works with Alexa through the app or a separate Alexa-enabled device — it has no microphone of its own. The ecobee Premium has Alexa and Siri built directly into the thermostat, plus compatibility with Google Assistant, Apple HomeKit, SmartThings and IFTTT, so it can field voice commands with no other device in the room. The Nest supports Google Assistant and is Matter certified, which matters for connecting it into a smart-home system that isn't locked to one ecosystem. The Nest and ecobee both list Bluetooth for setup and local control; the Amazon unit doesn't.

## Matching the specs to your home

- **No existing C-wire and you don't want to add an adapter kit** — the Nest is the one built to run without one; the other two either require it or need an extra kit to work around it.
- **You want voice control without buying a smart speaker** — built-in voice assistants are an ecobee Premium spec, not something the other two publish.
- **A different room runs hot or cold from the rest of the house** — a system that ships with a placeable room sensor, like the ecobee's included SmartSensor, is a published way to account for that without new wiring.
- **You want the lowest-complexity install and don't need a screen** — the Amazon Smart Thermostat's spec sheet is the shortest of the three.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does every smart thermostat need a C-wire?**

No. The Nest runs without one by design. The Amazon Smart Thermostat requires a C-wire or a separately purchased adapter kit, and the ecobee Premium requires one unless its included Power Extender Kit is installed instead — three different published answers to the same question.

**What does a built-in voice assistant add if I already own a smart speaker?**

Mainly redundancy and placement. If a speaker already covers the room, Alexa or Siri built into the thermostat doesn't add a new capability — you can already say the same commands to the speaker. It matters more in a room without one, or for voice control that keeps working if the speaker's connection or power ever drops.

**Is the included room sensor the same as the thermostat's own built-in sensors?**

No. The ecobee Premium's built-in sensors — temperature, humidity, proximity, occupancy, air quality — sit in the thermostat body and only read conditions where it's mounted. The included SmartSensor is a separate, placeable unit for a different room, reporting temperature and occupancy from wherever it's put rather than from the hallway a thermostat typically sits in.
