Robot vacuums are one of those categories where the $300 model and the $1,300 model both technically "clean your floors," but the actual day-to-day experience is very different. We looked at one option from each price tier to help you figure out where your money is actually going.

## What changes as the price goes up

The biggest differences between tiers aren't raw suction power — it's navigation intelligence, obstacle avoidance, and how much manual prep the robot needs before it can run unattended. Budget models tend to need you to clear cables and small objects off the floor first; premium models increasingly handle that themselves and build much more accurate maps of your home.

## If pet hair is your main problem

None of these three are pet-hair specialists — that's a separate category worth its own research if shedding is your primary concern. All three here have pet hair listed as a relative weak point compared to models built specifically for pet owners, so keep that in mind if you have dogs or cats.

## Which tier makes sense for you

If you have a small, relatively simple apartment layout, the budget pick handles the basics well and you probably won't notice most of what the premium tier adds. If you have a larger home with more furniture and clutter, the mid-range option's better obstacle handling starts to matter. The premium tier is really for people who want the closest thing to "never think about vacuuming again" — better mapping, better mopping, and less manual intervention, at a meaningfully higher price.

## Bottom line

Don't buy the most expensive robot vacuum assuming it'll solve problems the cheaper ones have (like pet hair) — check that the specific weak points of a model match what you actually need before paying the premium.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do robot vacuums work well on both carpet and hardwood?**

All three handle hardwood well since it's the easier surface. Carpet performance varies more — thicker or high-pile carpet reduces suction effectiveness across all price tiers, and none of these three are specifically marketed as high-pile carpet specialists, so expect somewhat reduced performance there regardless of which one you choose.

**How much maintenance do robot vacuums actually require?**

Expect to empty the dustbin regularly (weekly is typical for average homes), clean the brush roll of hair wrap monthly, and replace filters every few months. The premium tier often includes self-emptying base stations that reduce this frequency significantly — that convenience is part of what you're paying for at the higher price point.

**Will a robot vacuum work in a multi-level home?**

Yes, but it can't climb stairs, so you'll need to either carry it between floors or buy a separate unit per level if you want full automation on each floor. Mapping-based models (like the premium pick here) typically let you save separate maps for different levels if you do move it between floors.
