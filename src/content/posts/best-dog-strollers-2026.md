Dog strollers have moved well past a novelty for tiny lapdogs — search interest in the category has climbed as owners look for ways to keep aging, injured, or heat-sensitive dogs moving on walks and errands without carrying them the whole way. We compared three real options across price tiers, from a lightweight no-zip fold-up to a heavy-duty model rated for dogs pushing 100 pounds, based on published specs and owner reviews rather than a lab test.

## Pet Gear Happy Trails Lite No-Zip Pet Stroller

The Happy Trails Lite is built for small dogs and cats under 25 pounds, and its standout feature is the zipperless canopy — you lift the top panel to reach your pet instead of fighting a zipper track, which matters if you're doing it one-handed while holding a leash. The waterproof liner pulls out for washing, and front shock absorbers plus rear brakes make it usable on more than just smooth sidewalks. At 11.5 pounds it folds down easily for a car trunk. The tradeoff is capacity: at 25 pounds this only works for toy and small breeds, and some owner reviews note the wheels feel flimsier than the metal-frame models a tier up.

## Gen7Pets Promenade Pet Stroller

The Promenade steps up to a 50-pound weight limit, which covers most small-to-medium breeds — think beagles, corgis, or French bulldogs — while staying light enough at around 16 pounds to lift into a car. It has a one-button fold, a covered storage compartment underneath, and a two-cup parent tray, plus rear-wheel brakes and shock absorbers for uneven pavement or gravel paths. Mesh windows front and back keep airflow moving on warm days. The main complaints in owner reviews are wheel alignment drifting after heavy use and zippers that feel less sturdy than the no-zip design below it — reasonable wear for a stroller in this price range.

## WonderFold Hudson FlatFold Pet Stroller

The Hudson FlatFold is built for bigger dogs: the large size handles up to 99 pounds, and the medium size still covers 66 pounds, making this the option for dogs too big for a typical pet stroller. It folds flat to about 7.5 inches for trunk storage, has 360-degree swivel front wheels with a rear brake, a hidden privacy cover, and a built-in leash lock so a nervous or reactive dog can't slip out mid-walk. It's the heaviest stroller here when empty, at 22.5 to 27 pounds depending on size, and it costs meaningfully more than the other two — which only makes sense if you actually need the higher weight capacity.

## Bottom line

For a toy breed or a cat, the Happy Trails Lite covers the basics without overspending. Most small-to-medium dog owners will get more use out of the Promenade's 50-pound capacity and extra storage. If your dog is a larger breed recovering from surgery, dealing with arthritis, or simply too heavy for a standard stroller, the Hudson FlatFold is the one actually built to hold them.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are dog strollers only for small dogs?**

No — while most budget strollers cap out around 25 to 50 pounds, models like the WonderFold Hudson FlatFold are rated for dogs up to 99 pounds. Weight capacity is the first spec to check, since exceeding it can strain the frame and wheels over time. Large-breed strollers are typically bigger, heavier, and more expensive as a result.

**When does a dog actually need a stroller instead of a leash walk?**

Common reasons include recovering from surgery, arthritis or joint pain in senior dogs, short legs that can't keep pace on longer walks, or hot pavement that's unsafe for paws. Strollers also let owners bring dogs along on errands or trips where walking the full distance isn't realistic. For a lot of owners it functions as a mobility aid rather than a luxury item.

**What's the difference between a no-zip and a zippered stroller canopy?**

No-zip designs like the Happy Trails Lite use a lift-up panel instead of a zipper track, which is faster for one-handed access and has one less part that can jam or break. Zippered canopies, common on mid-range and premium strollers, tend to seal better against wind and rain. Neither is strictly better — it's a tradeoff between convenience and weather protection.
