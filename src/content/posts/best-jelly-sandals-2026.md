Jelly sandals have quietly become one of the season's most-searched shoe styles, and they're not just a nostalgia play anymore — the category now spans $25 beach basics and $100-plus designer-adjacent heels. We compared three tiers based on materials, brand reputation, and owner reviews to help you figure out which one actually fits how you'll wear them.

## Ipanema Fashion Jelly Sandals

Ipanema is a Brazilian brand that's been making jelly footwear since the 1970s, and this pair is the budget entry point into the category. They're molded from a scented, recyclable PVC blend rather than the stiff plastic older jellies are known for, and the footbed has enough give that owners report wearing them comfortably for a full day at the beach or around town. Based on buyer feedback, the main complaint is fit: the sandals run a touch narrow, so if you have wider feet it's worth sizing up half a size. They're not going to replace a proper walking sandal, but for the price they're a reasonable everyday option in a style that's genuinely trending right now.

## Melissa Beach Slide Jelly Sandals

Melissa is the brand most associated with modern jelly shoes — you'll see its scented, glossy PVC pieces show up in fashion coverage every summer. The Beach Slide is their straightforward slide silhouette, and compared to cheaper alternatives, owners and reviewers consistently point to the thicker material and the light tread pattern on the sole, which makes them noticeably less slippery on wet pool decks than basic jellies. The glossy finish also holds color better over a season of wear. The tradeoff is price: you're paying roughly double what a budget jelly costs, and because Melissa's collaborations and colorways are limited-run, the styles people want most tend to sell out early in the season.

## Melissa Possession Heel Jelly Sandals

For anyone who wants the jelly-shoe aesthetic without it reading as strictly a pool sandal, Melissa's heeled Possession line is the premium option. It uses the same recyclable PVC as the brand's flat styles but adds a block heel, which makes it wearable with an outfit rather than just swimwear — part of why jelly heels have been picking up search interest alongside the flat styles this year. Because Melissa frequently partners with designers on limited colorways, this line also tends to feel more "of the moment" than a plain slide. The obvious downside is that a PVC heel isn't built for standing or walking long distances the way leather or a cushioned sport sandal would be, and at this price point you're well past impulse-buy territory.

## Bottom line

If you want a comfortable, inexpensive pair to rotate through the summer, the Ipanema sandals cover the basics without much risk. The Melissa Beach Slide is the better buy if you want the style to actually last past one season, and the Possession Heel is worth it only if you specifically want a dressier jelly shoe rather than a pool sandal.

## Frequently asked questions

**Do jelly sandals run true to size?**
Based on owner reviews, most jelly sandals — including the Ipanema pair here — run slightly narrow and a little small, so many buyers size up half a size, especially if they have wider or higher-volume feet. Because PVC doesn't stretch the way leather does, it's worth checking the brand's specific size chart rather than assuming your usual size will fit.

**Are jelly sandals actually durable, or just a trend?**
The material itself, PVC, is very durable and water-resistant, which is why the style keeps coming back. Where sandals differ is construction quality: thicker, brand-name jellies like Melissa's tend to hold their shape and color longer than ultra-cheap versions, based on the specs and owner feedback we compared.

**Can you wear jelly sandals for more than just the beach?**
Yes — flat styles work fine for casual daytime wear, and heeled styles like the Possession are designed specifically to be dressed up. The main limitation is comfort over long periods on your feet, since PVC doesn't offer the cushioning or arch support that athletic or leather sandals do.
