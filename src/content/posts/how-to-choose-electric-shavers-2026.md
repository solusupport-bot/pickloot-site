Electric shavers all show the same handful of marketing words — "closer shave," "gentle on skin" — which makes them look interchangeable until you check what each manufacturer actually publishes. Four specs account for most of the real difference: battery runtime, whether the shaver is rated for wet or dry use, the blade or head configuration, and how fast it recharges. This guide walks through what each one changes, then lays three models side by side so you can match the numbers to your own bathroom routine.

## Battery runtime decides how often you think about charging, not shave quality

A longer runtime does not mean a closer or more comfortable shave — the cutting mechanism does that work. What runtime changes is how often the shaver has to go back on its stand. Braun publishes 50 minutes for the Series 5 and 60 minutes for the Series 9 Pro+, both on a full charge. The Philips model in this comparison does not publish a runtime figure at all, which typically means the manufacturer is not positioning that unit around cordless flexibility.

If you shave for two or three minutes most mornings, even the lower figure covers two to three weeks between full charges. The number mostly matters if you travel without a charger or let the shaver sit uncharged between uses.

## Wet/dry use changes where and how you can shave

This is a safety and convenience spec, not a performance one. A dry-only shaver is built for use on bare, dry skin — the electronics and seals are not rated for water or foam, and the manufacturer will say so plainly rather than leave it unstated. A wet/dry rating means the shaver is sealed against water, foam and gel, so it can be used in the shower.

Both Braun models here publish a 100% waterproof rating, with the Series 9 Pro+ going further and stating it is tested against up to 24 hours of submersion — a durability claim, not something you would ever need in daily use, but it indicates the sealing margin.

## Blade/head configuration is what actually touches your skin

This is where the tiers diverge the most. The Philips uses four flexing heads carrying 27 small blades plus a separate pop-up trimmer — a head-count-and-blade-count design aimed at following facial contours. The Braun Series 5 uses three flexible foil-and-blade elements tuned for speed. The Series 9 Pro+ adds a fifth cutting element and a sixth integrated trimmer, each doing a different job: two foils for the main cut, a lifting blade for flat-lying hairs, a protective bar, and a dedicated cutting blade.

More elements generally mean the manufacturer is targeting denser or coarser facial hair in fewer strokes, not that the shave is automatically closer for every face. This spec is also the hardest to compare directly across brands, so treat the counts as what each maker emphasizes rather than a ranking.

## Quick charge time is a convenience spec, not a performance one

Both Braun models publish the same quick-charge figure: five minutes for one shave. That is useful on a morning you forgot to charge, not a reason to pick one over the other since they match. The Philips page does not publish a quick-charge number, consistent with it skipping the runtime figure too — the two tend to be reported together or not at all.

## Matching the specs to your routine

- **You shave dry, standing at the sink** — the wet/dry rating does not change your decision, so the blade/head configuration and trimmer design matter more than the battery specs.
- **You shave in the shower** — a published wet/dry rating is the first filter; both Braun models here clear it, the Philips does not.
- **You travel often or forget to charge** — the quick-charge figure is the spec to check, since five minutes covering one shave removes most of the risk of a dead battery on travel days.
- **You have dense or coarse facial hair** — the blade/head configuration is the spec to weigh most heavily, since manufacturers add shaving elements specifically to cut more hair per pass.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a higher number of blades or shaving elements always mean a closer shave?**

Not in a way manufacturers quantify directly — "closeness" isn't a standardized published metric across brands, so a higher element count reflects engineering emphasis (handling denser hair in fewer passes) rather than a guaranteed result. Skin sensitivity and shaving technique affect the outcome as much as the hardware.

**Why does the budget model here not list a battery runtime?**

Manufacturers publish the specs they consider relevant to how a product is positioned. A shaver built and marketed as a dry-use, pop-up-trimmer model does not always get a runtime or charge-time figure on its spec page the way a wet/dry cordless model does. An unpublished spec means you cannot compare on that axis, not that the figure is necessarily poor.

**Is a 100% waterproof rating the same as being safe to use in the shower?**

As published by the manufacturers here, yes — a 100% waterproof rating on a wet/dry model specifically covers use with water, foam and gel. A dry-only shaver carries no such rating and is not built for that exposure, which is a distinction worth checking before it ever gets wet.
