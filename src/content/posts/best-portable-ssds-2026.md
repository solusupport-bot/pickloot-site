Memory and storage prices have jumped sharply through 2026 as a global DRAM and NAND shortage works through the supply chain, with reports of DDR5 kits up several times over and 1TB SSDs roughly doubling in cost compared to last year. Portable SSD prices are following the same trend, so buying before the next price update — rather than waiting — is the more expensive gamble right now. We compared three drives across budget, everyday, and high-capacity use cases.

## The budget pick for basic backups

The Crucial X9 is a straightforward, no-frills portable SSD that covers the most common use case: getting files off a laptop and onto something you can carry around. It skips the rugged housing and top-tier speed ratings that drive up the price on other drives, which is exactly why it's still one of the more reasonably priced 1TB options as shortage-driven pricing pushes the category upward. It's a reasonable pick if your files are documents and photos rather than large video projects.

## The everyday pick built to survive your bag

The Samsung T7 Shield earns its price premium over the X9 mainly through durability — the rubberized exterior and IP65 rating mean it can handle the kind of treatment a drive actually gets when it lives in a backpack or camera bag: drops, dust, the occasional rain-soaked commute. Based on Samsung's published specs and owner reviews, it also has enough sustained throughput to handle video editing workflows, not just file storage, which makes it the more versatile choice if you're not sure exactly how demanding your use case will get.

## The premium pick for large libraries

The Samsung T9 is the one to consider if 1TB isn't enough or if you specifically need the fastest transfer speeds on offer — its rated 2,000MB/s sequential speed is roughly double what the T7 Shield can hit, though you'll only see that number with a laptop or desktop that has a matching USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 port. For anyone maintaining a full archive of raw photo or video files, the extra capacity and headroom are worth the jump in price, especially while that price is only going to look better in hindsight given where the market is heading.

## Bottom line

Buy based on how you'll actually use the drive rather than chasing the top spec: the Crucial X9 covers basic backup needs at the lowest price, the Samsung T7 Shield adds real-world durability and speed for creative work, and the Samsung T9 is the move if you need 2TB or genuinely fast transfers. Given the current pricing environment, treat "buy now" as the safer default if you were going to need one of these within the next several months anyway.

## Frequently asked questions

**Why are portable SSD prices rising right now?**

Manufacturers have been reporting a global shortage of DRAM and NAND flash memory through 2026, driven partly by memory chipmakers redirecting production capacity toward AI hardware. That's tightened supply for consumer storage products across the board, and multiple industry trackers have reported SSD contract prices rising sharply as a result, with further increases forecast before supply catches up.

**Do I actually need a USB 3.2 Gen 2x2 port to benefit from the Samsung T9?**

You'll still get solid performance on an older or more common USB-C port, but you won't see the drive's full rated 2,000MB/s speed without a Gen 2x2-capable port on your laptop or desktop. Check your device's specs before paying the premium for the T9 specifically for its top-end speed — if your ports cap out lower, the T7 Shield may get you similar real-world performance for less.

**Is an IP65 rating actually worth paying more for?**

If the drive is going to live in a bag, get tossed in a backpack pocket, or travel with you regularly, yes — dust and light water exposure are common, low-drama ways external drives fail, and a sealed, rubberized housing like the T7 Shield's meaningfully reduces that risk. If the drive mostly stays on a desk at home, the protection matters less and a cheaper, unrated drive is a reasonable way to save money.
