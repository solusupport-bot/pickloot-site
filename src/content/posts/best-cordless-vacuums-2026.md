Cordless vacuum searches have climbed sharply in 2026 as more households retire their corded uprights and robot vacuums for something that can handle stairs, cars, and quick cleanups in one pass. We compared specs and owner feedback across three price tiers to see where the real differences show up: battery swaps, suction ratings, and how much maintenance each model asks for. None of these are robot vacuums, so there is no scheduling or mapping involved — you push the button and clean the way you always have, just without a cord to work around.

## Electrolux Well Q7 Cordless Vacuum

The Well Q7 is the pick for anyone who wants a genuinely light stick vacuum without stepping into premium pricing. It converts into a handheld unit for quick trips out to the car or over a couch, and its filtration setup holds up well for a budget-tier machine. Based on owner reviews and published specs, the tradeoff is capacity: the dustbin is on the smaller side, so larger cleanups mean emptying it more than once. Suction also softens on thicker carpet, which makes this a better match for hard floors, low-pile rugs, and everyday pickup rather than deep periodic cleans.

## Samsung Jet 75 Pet Cordless Vacuum

Sitting in the middle of the lineup, the Jet 75 Pet is built around pet hair and everyday debris. Its suction is rated in the 200AW class, which is enough to pull hair out of carpet fibers without much visible drag, and the swappable battery means a dead pack does not end the cleaning session the way fixed batteries can. It skips the auto-empty dock found on higher-end models, so you are manually emptying the bin between sessions, and it can clog if you run it directly over larger debris like cereal or litter without a slower pass first. For most multi-pet households, it is the tier that balances price and performance most closely.

## Tineco Pure One A90S Cordless Vacuum

The A90S sits at the top of this lineup with suction specs around 270AW, aimed at deep carpet and larger homes where a lighter machine would need more passes. Its debris sensors read the floor and adjust power automatically, and the zero-tangle brush head is designed specifically to cut down on the hair-wrap problem that slows down cheaper stick vacuums over time. What you do not get at this price is an auto-empty base, which is a notable omission compared to some competitors in the same range, and the body runs heavier than the two models above it. It is best suited to buyers who prioritize raw suction and smart sensing over dock convenience.

## Bottom line

If you want the lightest, most affordable option for daily pickup, the Well Q7 covers the basics well. The Jet 75 Pet is the strongest all-around choice for pet owners who need reliable suction without paying flagship prices, and the Pure One A90S makes sense if deep carpet cleaning and top-tier suction matter more to you than dock automation. Match the tier to your flooring rather than your budget alone — a mostly hard-floor apartment rarely needs the top model, while a house full of deep-pile carpet and shedding pets usually outgrows the entry-level one within a year.

## Frequently asked questions

**Are cordless vacuums powerful enough to replace a corded upright?**

For most homes, yes, especially on hard floors and low-to-medium pile carpet. Owners moving from corded uprights generally report the difference is more noticeable on thick, deep-pile carpet, where higher-AW models like the Pure One A90S close most of the gap.

**How long do cordless vacuum batteries actually last?**

Runtime specs typically range from around 20 to 60 minutes depending on suction mode, but real-world use skews shorter on max power. Models with swappable batteries, like the Jet 75 Pet, avoid the mid-clean shutdown issue that fixed-battery vacuums can run into in larger homes.

**Is it worth paying extra for an auto-empty dock?**

It depends on how much you value hands-off maintenance versus raw suction per dollar. None of the three vacuums here include one, which is common below the very top of the market, and all three still empty in a few seconds by hand — it is a convenience tradeoff, not a performance one.
