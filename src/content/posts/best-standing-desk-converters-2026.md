Desk converters all promise the same thing: standing without replacing your desk. The published specifications tell you how differently they go about it. Four numbers account for nearly all of the day-to-day difference - the lift mechanism, the height range, the weight the frame is rated to lift, and the size of the worksurface it puts on your desk. This guide explains what each one changes, then lays three models out side by side.

## The lift mechanism decides how much friction switching costs

This is the fork in the category. A manual converter uses a gas spring you release and push, which means no cord, no motor, and nothing to fail electrically. An electric converter uses a motor and a button.

The spec matters in proportion to how often you switch. Once or twice a day, a spring-assisted lift is a few seconds of effort. Six or eight times a day, that effort is the reason people stop switching. Each type publishes its own durability claim: FlexiSpot states a 10% duty cycle for the EM7 - two minutes on, eighteen off - while VIVO states a 10,000-cycle rating for its gas spring.

## Height adjustment range is measured two different ways

Read this spec carefully, because manufacturers do not state it consistently. VIVO and FlexiSpot publish *travel* - how far the surface moves from its lowest position, 4.5 to 20 inches and 5.7 to 19.7 inches respectively. UPLIFT publishes both travel and absolute height: 21 inches of movement, from 21.5 inches to roughly 43.5 inches off the floor.

Travel alone cannot tell you whether a converter reaches your standing height - that depends on how tall your desk already is. Add maximum travel to your desk height and compare it against your standing elbow height. The lowest position matters in the other direction: a unit that only drops to 4.5 inches leaves the keyboard permanently raised when you sit.

## Weight capacity is two numbers, not one

The headline figure is what the lift mechanism is rated to raise: 33 lb, 55 lb, and 110 lb across these three. That spread is wide enough to be a real constraint rather than a safety margin. Two 27-inch monitors on a dual arm plus a laptop clears 33 lb without difficulty.

The second number is easier to miss. VIVO publishes a separate 4 lb limit for the keyboard tray, and UPLIFT a 17.6 lb rating for the VESA monitor mount. A converter can sit within its overall capacity and still be over a sub-limit, so where a monitor arm is involved the VESA figure is the one that governs.

## Worksurface size and unit weight are the desk-fit constraints

The worksurface is what sits on your existing desk, so its depth is the number to measure against. These three run from 15.7 to 25 inches deep - the deepest needs a desk of roughly 30 inches to sit safely with clearance behind it, which many home desks do not have.

Unit weight then decides whether the converter is furniture or an accessory. At 37 lb one person can reposition it; at 92 lb it is a two-person job and effectively permanent. One of the three does not publish a unit weight at all. An unpublished spec is not a bad spec, but it does mean you cannot compare on that axis before it arrives.

## Matching the specs to your desk

Rather than a single answer, the useful question is which constraint binds hardest.

- **Your desk is shallow (under about 24 inches deep)** - worksurface depth is the spec to optimise, and the shallowest option here is the manual one.
- **You switch positions many times a day** - the lift mechanism, since that is where the friction lives.
- **You run monitor arms or dual displays** - weight capacity, and specifically the VESA sub-rating where one is published.
- **Your desk is unusually tall or short, or you move the setup around** - the height range, calculated against your own desk, and the unit weight.

## Frequently asked questions

**Will a desk converter fit on any desk?**

Most converters need at least as much desk depth as the converter's own worksurface, plus a few inches of clearance behind it for the lifting frame. Measure your desk before buying, especially against the deeper models - a desk under about 24 inches deep will not comfortably take a full-size converter.

**How much weight can these actually hold?**

The published lifting capacities across these three are 33 lb, 55 lb and 110 lb. Check the sub-limits too: keyboard trays and VESA monitor mounts carry their own published ratings that are much lower than the headline figure, and those are what a dual-monitor setup runs into first.

**Is standing all day actually better than sitting?**

Standing all day isn't the goal - alternating between sitting and standing is what's generally recommended, since standing motionless for hours has its own downsides in leg fatigue and joint strain. A converter's value is in making that switching easy, which is why the lift mechanism spec tends to matter more than any other one on the sheet.
