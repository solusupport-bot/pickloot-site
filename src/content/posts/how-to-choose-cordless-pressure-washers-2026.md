Every cordless pressure washer promises to replace a hose. What actually separates them are four published numbers: how much force the pump generates, how much water it moves, what battery platform it runs on, and how long it runs before you're swapping packs. None of the three units compared here publish all four cleanly - two are sold as tool-only, and one skips flow rate and runtime entirely - which is itself useful information before you buy.

## Pressure (PSI) decides what the water can break loose

PSI measures the force behind the spray, and it's the number that determines whether water alone lifts dried mud off a tire or just beads off it. The three units here span a wide range - 415 PSI to 3,000 PSI - and that range matters more at the low end than the high end: below roughly 1,000 PSI, you're closer to a strong hose attachment than a cleaning tool, which is exactly how WORX frames the Hydroshot. Above that, higher PSI mostly shortens how long you dwell on a stubborn spot, and it raises the risk of stripping paint or gouging softer wood if you hold the nozzle too close.

## Flow rate (GPM) is what actually rinses the dirt away

PSI loosens dirt; gallons per minute is what carries it off the surface afterward. A high-pressure, low-flow stream can blast a concentrated spot clean while leaving a dirty film spread around it, because there isn't enough water moving to flush the loosened grime away. Both the RYOBI and the Greenworks publish separate GPM figures for their high-power and ECO modes, and the drop is proportional to the pressure drop - ECO mode isn't a weaker version of the same spray, it's a genuinely different pressure-and-flow combination. WORX doesn't publish a GPM figure at all for the Hydroshot, which is consistent with its positioning as a light-duty cleaner rather than a surface-stripping tool.

## Battery voltage governs sustained power and which packs you can reuse

Voltage is a rough proxy for how much power the motor can draw continuously, which is why the 60V Greenworks posts the highest PSI and GPM of the three and the 20V WORX the lowest. It also decides your battery ecosystem: the RYOBI and Greenworks units here are sold tool-only, so the real cost and the real runtime depend on which battery you add from that platform. If you already own packs in one of these voltage families, that compatibility is worth more than a few hundred extra PSI on a competing brand.

## Runtime is a function of mode, not a fixed number

Only two of the three units publish runtime, and both attach a caveat. RYOBI's roughly 40-minute figure assumes a 40V 12Ah battery that isn't included in the box. Greenworks publishes two numbers - up to 18 minutes at full power, up to 25 in ECO mode - which makes the mode choice a direct trade between pressure and endurance, not just a quieter setting. WORX publishes no runtime figure at all, only a relative claim that its included 4.0Ah pack roughly doubles the runtime of a smaller 2.0Ah one.

## Matching the specs to your driveway, deck or garage

- **The jobs are small and scattered** (bikes, outdoor furniture, wheel wells) - the lowest PSI unit covers this, and skipping a separate battery purchase matters more than headroom you won't use.
- **You're already inside a battery platform** - voltage compatibility with tools you own outweighs a higher PSI number on a brand you'd have to start from zero with.
- **The grime is actually baked on** (driveway stains, a fence, a deck) - the highest published PSI-and-GPM combination clears it faster, and a sub-20-minute full-power runtime is the cost of that.
- **You need to run longer than one battery allows** - check the ECO-mode runtime figure specifically, since that's the number that describes a longer, lower-pressure session rather than the headline spec.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a cordless pressure washer replace a corded or gas one for a full driveway?**

At the top of this range - 3,000 PSI and 2.0 GPM - it's in the same territory as many light-duty corded electric washers, but published runtimes under 20 minutes at full power mean a full driveway will likely need a battery swap partway through, which a corded unit never requires.

**Why do two of these three not include a battery?**

Tool-only pricing is standard across 40V and 60V yard-tool platforms, because the assumption is you already own packs for other tools on that voltage. If you're starting from zero on a platform, the real cost and the real runtime both depend on which battery you add, so check that before comparing price.

**Does a higher PSI number always mean more cleaning power?**

Not on its own. PSI without enough GPM to flush the loosened dirt away can leave a surface streaked rather than clean, which is why the manufacturers here publish pressure and flow together for each mode rather than pressure alone.
