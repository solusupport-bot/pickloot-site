Cable management is one of those things that looks purely cosmetic until you actually deal with a desk where you can't find the right cord, a power strip is dangling by its own weight, and vacuuming under the desk is a hazard. We looked at three ways to fix it, starting from "just tidy this up cheaply" to "I want this desk to look like it has no cables at all."

## Start here if you've never organized your cables before

The Amazon Basics kit is the right starting point for almost anyone. It's inexpensive, covers the basic problem (loose cables running everywhere), and gives you clips, ties, and a Velcro roll to figure out what your setup actually needs before spending more. The main limitation is thicker cables and power bricks — the small clips aren't built for those.

## The mid-range tray solves the power strip problem

If your actual issue is a power strip and a tangle of cords hanging visibly under the desk, the 40-inch under-desk tray is the fix. It requires drilling a few screws into the underside of your desk, which is a bit more commitment than the clip kit, but it's the difference between "cables are tied together" and "cables are completely out of sight."

## When the premium option is worth it

The clip-mount mesh tray costs more for a similar function to the drilled tray, but the tradeoff is installation — it clips on instead of requiring screws, which matters if you're renting, using a standing desk that changes height, or just don't want to drill into your furniture. If that flexibility matters to you, it's worth the premium; if not, the cheaper drilled tray does the same job for less.

## Bottom line

Most people should start with a basic clip-and-tie kit before buying a tray — you'll quickly find out whether your real problem is loose cables (kit solves it) or a visible power strip and cord bundle (a tray solves it). Buy the tray that matches how you're willing to mount it: screws for the cheaper option, clips for the pricier no-drill version.
