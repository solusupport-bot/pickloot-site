Hurricane season runs roughly June through November across the US, and coastal and inland states alike see multi-day outages from storms, heat-driven grid strain, or the occasional ice event. A portable power station isn't a full-home generator, but it closes the gap between "phone's dead" and "the fridge is warm" without gas, fumes, or a permit. We compared three tiers by capacity, output, and what each is actually built to run — not by how loud the marketing is.

## Bluetti Elite 30 V2 — the grab-and-go option

At 288Wh and 600W output, the Elite 30 V2 is sized for the outage that lasts a few hours to a day, not a week. It'll keep a router and a couple of lamps alive, charge phones and a laptop, and run a small fan — enough to stay connected and see what you're doing. Based on published specs, it uses LiFePO4 cells, the same chemistry found in higher-capacity units, so it should hold up to repeated charge cycles better than older lithium-ion packs. The tradeoff is capacity: there's no keeping a fridge cold or a CPAP machine running overnight on 288Wh, and the base kit doesn't include a solar panel, so recharging mid-outage means finding AC power somewhere.

## EcoFlow Delta 3 Plus — the middle tier that covers most households

The Delta 3 Plus roughly triples the budget option's capacity at 1024Wh, with a 2400W output rating that opens the door to small appliances and some corded power tools, not just electronics. Reviewers have flagged its AC recharge speed as a standout, which matters in practice — a station that takes all day to refill is less useful if outages come in waves. It also pairs with an app for tracking remaining runtime, useful for deciding what to power down before you're guessing. It's a noticeably bigger, heavier unit than the budget tier, so it's built to stay near an outlet rather than travel in a backpack, and for a household that only needs to keep a router and a lamp on, it's more capacity than necessary.

## Anker Solix F3800 — for households that treat outages as a recurring problem

At 3840Wh and 6000W output, with the option to expand capacity further with add-on batteries, the Solix F3800 is in a different category from the other two — it's specced and marketed for whole-home backup and RV use, the kind of purchase made by households in a region where multi-day outages happen most years rather than once in a while. That output can carry major appliances, not just electronics, through an extended outage. The cost is real: this is a four-figure, closet-or-garage-sized purchase, not something you toss in a bag on the way out the door, and it needs a real storage spot and setup time before an emergency hits, not during one.

## Bottom line

If you just want peace of mind for a short outage — router, lights, phone charging — the Bluetti Elite 30 V2 covers it without much cost or bulk. Most households in storm-prone areas will get the most practical value from something in the EcoFlow Delta 3 Plus's range: real appliance-level output without the price or footprint of a whole-home system. The Anker Solix F3800 only makes sense if outages are a recurring, multi-day reality where you live and you're ready to treat backup power as household infrastructure rather than an emergency add-on.

## Frequently asked questions

**Can a portable power station run a full-size refrigerator?**
Smaller units like the Bluetti Elite 30 V2 can start a fridge but won't sustain it for long at 288Wh. Mid-range and premium stations with 1000Wh-plus capacity and higher continuous output ratings, like the EcoFlow Delta 3 Plus or Anker Solix F3800, are better matched to a fridge's cycling power draw over several hours.

**Do I need a solar panel to make one useful during a long outage?**
Not for a short outage, since you can charge from AC power beforehand and again once power returns. For outages that stretch multiple days, a compatible solar panel extends usefulness significantly, but check compatibility and panel wattage against the specific station — panels aren't universal across brands.

**Is a portable power station a substitute for a whole-home generator?**
Generally no, unless you're looking at the largest, most expensive tier with expansion batteries. A portable power station is quieter, safer indoors, and needs no fuel, but a gas or propane whole-home generator still outperforms it on sustained output for multi-day, whole-house needs.
