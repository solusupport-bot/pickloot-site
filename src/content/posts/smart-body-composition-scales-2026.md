Smart body composition scales all run the same basic trick -- a weak electrical current and bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) to estimate body fat, muscle, and water on top of a plain weight reading -- but three published spec sheets from well-known brands show real differences in how many metrics they report, how a reading gets from the scale to your phone, how many people in a household the scale can tell apart, and what keeps it running day to day.

## Metrics tracked ranges wider than the core BIA numbers suggest

Every scale in this category reports weight, body fat percentage, muscle mass, and water percentage at minimum, since that's what a single BIA sensor pair produces. Where they differ is how many additional numbers get derived from that same reading and surfaced in the app -- bone mass, BMI, and other calculated stats stacked on top of the same raw measurement. A higher metric count on a spec sheet isn't a sign of extra hardware in most cases; it's the manufacturer's software deriving more numbers from the same underlying impedance reading, so treat the count as a feature-richness spec rather than a precision spec.

## Connectivity decides whether a weigh-in syncs itself or waits on your phone

A WiFi-connected scale pushes each reading to the app on its own, which means your phone doesn't need to be in the room, let alone unlocked, for the data to log. A Bluetooth-only scale needs your phone nearby and the app open (or at least backgrounded and paired) to capture a reading in real time -- step on a Bluetooth-only scale with your phone in another room and you may not get a synced number at all. This is a bigger day-to-day difference than it looks on paper, especially for a scale that's going to live in a bathroom where phones don't usually live too.

## Max users stored determines whether the scale actually knows who's standing on it

Multi-user support means the scale (or more precisely, the app) tries to recognize who just stepped on based on weight and impedance patterns and log the reading to the right profile automatically. The published limits vary by brand and, in at least one case we found, by which page on the brand's own site you're reading -- one official page for a model promised unlimited users while a separate FAQ from the same company capped "many models" at 16. If exact profile limits matter for your household, the safest move is cross-checking the number against the scale's own app listing rather than trusting a single marketing page.

## Power source is a bigger factor than it looks for a WiFi scale specifically

All three scales here run on AAA batteries rather than a rechargeable cell, which keeps the scale from ever needing a charging cable but means battery life is entirely about how often the scale talks to your network. A WiFi scale wakes up its radio and pushes a sync on every single use, which draws more power than a Bluetooth-only scale that mostly stays idle between weigh-ins -- and the two WiFi-capable scales in this comparison don't even agree with themselves on expected battery life, with one brand listing two different numbers on two of its own pages. Don't assume a quoted battery-life figure is the floor; treat it as one brand's average case.
