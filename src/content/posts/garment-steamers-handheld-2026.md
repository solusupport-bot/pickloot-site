Handheld garment steamers all look similar once you take the marketing photos away -- a wand, a water tank, a cord -- but the published spec sheets for three well-known models turn up real, measurable differences in exactly four numbers: wattage, tank capacity, heat-up time, and continuous steam time. The catch is that brands don't all publish that last one the same way, which matters more than it sounds like it should once you're standing in front of an empty tank mid-ironing. This guide walks through what each spec actually changes, then lines the three up side by side.

## Wattage tells you heat-up speed, not steam volume

Published wattage ranges from 1000W to nearly 1900W across handheld steamers, and the gap mostly buys you a faster heat-up, not more total steam over the life of a tank -- that's set by the tank size below. A higher-wattage unit gets to steaming sooner after you plug it in, which matters if you're steaming one shirt before you walk out the door. It matters less if you're working through a full load, where tank refills become the bigger time cost than the initial heat-up. It's also worth noting that wattage figures for the same model name sometimes disagree between retailer listings -- one unit in this category is listed as both 1550W and 1500W on the same page -- so a published number is a useful approximation rather than a guaranteed lab-tested figure.

## Water tank capacity is the real limiter on how long you can iron

Tank sizes in this category run small -- under 220 ml across every model we looked at -- which is the tradeoff for keeping a handheld steamer light enough to hold up and away from your body for several minutes at a time. A bigger tank means fewer trips back to the sink, but it also adds weight to a unit you're holding in one hand the whole time. None of these manufacturers publish how that weight changes as the tank empties, so treat the "full" weight on the spec sheet as the heaviest the unit will ever feel.

## Heat-up time adds up if you're steaming multiple garments

A 15-second difference in heat-up time looks trivial on a spec sheet, but if you're steaming five shirts back-to-back and the unit cools down between uses, that gap repeats every time. Heat-up specs are also measured from a cold start in a manufacturer's test lab; a unit that's already warm from recent use will be faster than its published number, and one that's sat unused for days may run slightly slower while it builds initial pressure.

## Continuous steam time isn't published the same way by every brand

This is the spec where shopping gets harder than it should be. Some brands publish a straightforward per-fill runtime in minutes. Others publish a steam output rate in grams per minute and leave you to divide it into the tank size yourself -- and at least one brand in this category publishes neither, listing only a maximum continuous-use safety limit that has nothing to do with how long a single tank actually lasts. If a listing doesn't give you a runtime number, assume it hasn't been measured for you and budget for refilling more often than the competitors that do publish one.
