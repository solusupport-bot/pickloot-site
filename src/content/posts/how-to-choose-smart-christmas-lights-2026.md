Smart string lights all promise the same thing: plug in, open an app, and the porch or the tree glows in whatever color you pick. What separates one model from another rarely shows up in the product photo. Four published specs account for most of it: LEDs per length, power draw, weatherproofing, and the protocol it uses to talk to your phone.

## LED count and string length set point density, not total brightness

None of these pages publish a lumens figure for the whole string — Hue and Twinkly don't list lumens at all, and Govee's lumens-per-bulb figure appears only on its separate permanent-outdoor-lighting line, not this model. Without a brightness number, the figure worth comparing is LED count against string length.

Twinkly and the Hue Festavia both publish 250 LEDs across 65.6 ft, roughly one LED every 3.1–3.15 inches — a dense, even glow. Govee's page gives the length (up to 144 ft) but not the bulb count for that string, so its spacing can't be calculated from published numbers alone. That's an unknown spec, not a worse one, and it's worth checking before assuming density matches the two denser strings.

## Power draw tracks LED count and driver design, not how bright it looks

The Hue Festavia draws a published 14 W for its 250-LED, 65.6 ft run. Govee's 144 ft string draws 54 W. The lengths differ, but even scaled per foot the gap doesn't close: Hue works out to about 0.21 W per foot against Govee's 0.37 W per foot. That difference comes down to driver efficiency and how the controller handles full-brightness RGB output — not visible brightness, since a shopper can't see wattage, only the result.

Twinkly's page is a reminder that a watt figure isn't always the string's actual draw: it lists a 36 W adapter output, the power supply's maximum capacity, not measured consumption. Treat an adapter rating and a consumption rating as different numbers unless a page says otherwise.

## The weatherproof rating is two digits, and only one is about water

An IP rating has two digits: the first covers solid-object and dust intrusion, the second covers water. IP44 (Twinkly) means protected against objects larger than 1 mm and water splashed from any direction — fine under an eave or on a covered porch, less fine in a snowbank or on wet ground. IP54 (Hue Festavia) steps dust protection up to "dust-protected" and keeps the same splash-proof water rating. IP65 (Govee) is dust-tight and rated for low-pressure water jets from any direction, the most tolerant of the three of driving rain or slush underfoot.

Worth noting on the Hue page specifically: its own FAQ states outdoor use applies only to units manufactured during or after 2023 — a 2022 unit of the same product name is indoor-only. The IP number on the box only tells the current story if the unit matches the date range the manufacturer attaches to it.

## Connectivity protocol decides whether you need a hub, and what still works offline

Govee and Twinkly both connect directly over 2.4 GHz Wi-Fi plus Bluetooth, so the app talks to the lights with no other hardware. The Hue Festavia uses Bluetooth and Zigbee with no native Wi-Fi radio; basic on/off, color, and dimming work over Bluetooth alone, but Matter, Alexa, and most scheduling route through a Hue Bridge. A household already running a Bridge gains the most from that model; one with none is adding hardware the other two don't require.

## Matching the specs to your yard or porch

- **You're lighting one long run, like a roofline or driveway** — check string length first, and whether LED count is published at that length.
- **The lights sit low, near snow, puddles, or sprinklers** — the water digit in the IP rating matters more than the dust digit; look for a 5 or higher.
- **You already have a smart-home hub** (Hue Bridge, SmartThings, a Matter controller) — connectivity protocol decides whether the lights slot in natively or need a separate app.
- **You're running several strings off one outdoor outlet** — power draw, not brightness, is what you're budgeting against the circuit.

## Frequently asked questions

**Does a higher IP rating mean the lights are also brighter or more color-accurate?**

No. The IP rating covers ingress protection against dust and water only — it says nothing about lumens, color range, or app features. An IP65-rated string and an IP44-rated string can have identical light output; the rating is purely about what weather the housing is sealed against.

**Will any of these still turn on if my home Wi-Fi goes down?**

The spec table can't fully answer this, since it lists protocols, not failure behavior. Govee and Twinkly depend on Wi-Fi for app and voice control, so a router outage typically blocks remote control, though physical buttons on the controller usually still work. The Hue Festavia's Bluetooth connection lets the Hue app control it directly from a nearby phone even without a working Bridge or internet — it's the Bridge-routed features that go down, not local Bluetooth control.

**Why don't any of these list total lumens for the string?**

It isn't an oversight unique to one brand — none of the three manufacturers publish a whole-string lumens figure on these product pages. Lumens-per-bulb appears on some manufacturers' other lighting lines, but not consistently here, which is why LED count and spacing are the closest published proxy for perceived brightness in this comparison.
